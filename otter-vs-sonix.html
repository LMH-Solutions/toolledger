Otter.ai vs Sonix (2026): live meeting notes or uploaded-file transcription? Pricing and limits compared

Research-based comparison. Prices and plan details read from otter.ai/pricing and sonix.ai/pricing on November 18, 2026 (US Eastern). Not hands-on tested. We have not measured either tool's accuracy. Prices are in USD and change; confirm on each vendor's page before you buy.

Otter.ai and Sonix both turn speech into text, and both can handle meetings and uploaded recordings. But each is built around a different job, and each charges in a different way:

  • Otter.ai is a meeting notetaker. It joins Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet calls (or records in its app), transcribes live and writes summaries. Plans are per user, with a monthly cap on minutes, a cap on how long one conversation can be, and a cap on how many recorded files you can import.
  • Sonix is a transcription platform for files you upload: interviews, podcasts, videos, recorded meetings. It adds an in-browser editor, translation, subtitles and AI analysis. Plans are based on hours of audio: $10 an hour pay-as-you-go, or a monthly bundle of hours.

The short version: for live meetings, Otter is far cheaper per person. For a steady stream of recorded files, long recordings, or languages other than the six Otter lists, Otter's limits start to bite, and Sonix fits better. The numbers are below.

The key difference: capped minutes per user vs paid hours of audio

With Otter you pay per person, and each plan caps three things: total minutes a month (on Basic and Pro), the length of a single conversation (30 minutes, 90 minutes, then 4 hours), and how many recorded files you can import. For people who mostly sit in meetings, those caps are generous for the price. Business lifts the meeting cap entirely.

With Sonix you pay for audio. Every hour you upload counts against your plan's hours, and anything past them is $10 an hour. Sonix's pricing page doesn't list a per-file length limit or a monthly file count; the limit is hours. Adding teammates costs extra but doesn't add hours.

So the right choice depends on where your audio comes from (live calls or finished files), how long each recording is, and which language it's in.

Sonix advertises "99% accuracy" in its marketing, and both vendors make accuracy claims. We haven't tested either, so we don't repeat those as facts. Both have free options (Otter Basic, Sonix's 30 free minutes), so try the same recording in each before paying.

Which one should you pick?

  • You want notes from your own Zoom, Meet or Teams calls: Otter. Start on Basic, then Pro ($8.33 per user annually) if you need longer meetings or more minutes.
  • Your meetings run longer than 90 minutes: Otter Business (4-hour cap) or Sonix. Compare $19.99 per user for Otter Business against your monthly hours on Sonix.
  • You transcribe interviews, podcasts or videos you already recorded: Sonix, especially if you need subtitles, translation or a careful editing pass.
  • Your audio isn't in English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese or Chinese: Sonix, which lists 54+ languages.
  • Only a few files a year: Sonix Pay As You Go ($10/hr, no subscription). Otter's free plan allows just 3 imports, ever.
