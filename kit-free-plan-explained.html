Kit free plan explained (2026): what the Newsletter plan includes, its limits, and when to upgrade

Research-based guide. Everything here was read from Kit's live pricing page and three Kit Help Center articles (US English, USD) on November 24, 2026 (US Eastern). Not hands-on tested. Plans change, so confirm on kit.com/pricing before you sign up.

Kit, the email platform formerly called ConvertKit, has one of the largest free plans among creator email tools: up to 10,000 subscribers at $0. That's why "Kit free plan" and "ConvertKit free plan limits" are common searches. The catch isn't list size. It's what the free plan can't do, mainly automated emails.

A naming note: Kit's pricing page and Help Center call it the Free plan, but the Creator plan card on the same page says "Everything in Newsletter plan, plus," so you may see it called the Newsletter plan too. They're the same plan. This guide covers what it includes, where its limits are, and when upgrading makes sense. For every paid price tier, see our Kit pricing explained (2026) guide. We haven't run a Kit account for this article. It reports what Kit publishes and points out where Kit's own pages disagree.

What the free plan includes

Kit's Help Center article "The Kit Free plan" (dated September 23, 2026) and the comparison table on the pricing page list these features as included on Free:

  • Audience growth: unlimited landing pages and opt-in forms, landing page and form templates, a newsletter feed and website, a customizable Kit domain or your own custom domain for landing pages, automatic delivery of an opt-in freebie (lead magnet file), and list growth reporting.
  • Creator Network: Free Recommendations and Smart Recommendations. The Help Center says you get five Smart Recommendation slots that fill automatically with similar creators, and you can be featured in other creators' recommendations. Paid plans get priority, so Kit says you'll be featured more often after upgrading. You can turn Recommendations off.
  • Subscribers: tagging, segmentation and an unsubscribe page builder.
  • Sending: unlimited broadcasts that you can send or schedule, the email template library, custom HTML/CSS templates, an image library, Liquid conditional content, one-click resend to people who didn't open, and A/B testing of two subject lines.
  • Selling: digital products, paid newsletters, recurring subscriptions and virtual tip jars.
  • Other: content snippets, API access, free apps in the Kit App Store (per the Help Center), and Kit University courses.

One detail that matters for lead magnets: the "deliver opt-in incentives" feature is marked as included on Free. So you can hand out a freebie automatically when someone signs up, even though a follow-up welcome sequence needs a paid plan.

The limits that actually matter

  • 10,000 subscribers. The Help Center says that when you pass 10,000 you'll need to upgrade to keep sending. Everything you built stays, and paid features unlock when you upgrade. Kit's billing article says it bills on confirmed, active subscribers, so cleaning out inactive subscribers can keep you under the line.
  • No automated emails. This is the big one. On Free, every email is a broadcast you send or schedule yourself. If your plan is "someone downloads my freebie, then gets five emails over two weeks," that's a sequence, and it needs Creator.
  • Kit branding. It stays on your landing pages, emails and product pages until you pay.
  • One login. If an assistant or co-founder needs their own access, that's a paid-plan feature.
  • Transaction fees. Selling on Free costs the same as on paid plans: 3.5% + 30¢ per sale, which Kit says includes card processing. On a $20 product that's $1.00 ($0.70 + $0.30).

Where Kit's own pages disagree

  • Sending limit: the pricing FAQ says "There are no sending limits on any of our plans." But the refund section of the Help Center's "Billing at Kit" article says that after you cancel, your account moves to the free plan, "which has fewer features and a lower sending limit." Kit doesn't state a number anywhere we looked.
  • A/B testing: the Creator plan card lists "A/B testing subject lines" as something Creator adds, but the comparison table and the Help Center both say Free includes A/B testing of two subject lines.
  • Plan names: Free is called the "Newsletter plan" on the Creator card, and the Help Center refers to the paid plans as "Creator and Creator Pro" while the pricing page says "Pro."
  • Integrations: the pricing table marks "100+ direct apps" as not available on Free, while the Help Center says only paid apps need a paid plan and free apps are still available to Free accounts.

None of these change the main picture, but if one of them decides it for you, ask Kit support before relying on it.

When to upgrade

This follows from the limits above. It isn't based on our own testing.

  • Stay on Free if you send a regular newsletter by hand, have fewer than 10,000 subscribers, work alone, and don't mind Kit branding. You can even start selling a digital product or a paid newsletter.
  • Move to Creator when you want an automatic welcome sequence, a lead-magnet funnel, RSS-to-email for a blog, app integrations, a second user, removed branding, or income from Paid Recommendations and sponsorships.
  • Look at Pro only if you need team seats beyond two, deeper reporting, Facebook audience sync or a referral program. Our pricing guide compares the two.

You don't have to pay to find out. Kit's pricing page says Creator and Pro each come with a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. When the trial ends you add a card to continue, or stay on the Free plan.

Upgrading, the current offer, and going back to Free

  • Price: Creator is listed at $39/mo and Pro at $79/mo for 1,000 subscribers billed monthly. The Help Center says annual billing gives "2 months free" (12 months for the price of 10), which the pricing page labels "save 17%." Prices rise with list size. See our tier-by-tier table.
  • Black Friday offer (shown on November 24, 2026): the pricing page's monthly view was labelled "Black Friday offer" and showed Creator at $20/mo for the first 3 months, then $39/mo, at 1,000 subscribers. The fine print says it "applies to the monthly Creator plan," gives 50% off for 3 months for accounts up to 500k subscribers, and "Ends Dec. 1." We didn't check the annual view's prices today. Earlier this month the page showed a separate first-year discount on yearly Pro, said to end November 30; check the live page for what's offered when you buy.
  • Going back to Free: the Help Center says you can switch to Free anytime from billing settings. On a paid plan you keep paid features until the end of the billing period. On a trial, paid access ends as soon as you switch. Automations and sequences you built stay in the account but stop running until you upgrade again. Downgrades aren't refunded or pro-rated.
  • Refunds: Kit says new customers can get a full refund within 30 days of their first payment, on monthly or annual plans.
