Sonix free trial explained (2026): the 30 free minutes, the credit card question, and what happens after

Research-based guide. Everything here was read from Sonix's live pricing page, FAQ, sign-up page and Sonix Help Center articles (US English, USD) on November 25, 2026 (US Eastern). Not hands-on tested: we haven't run a Sonix trial for this article and make no accuracy claims of our own. Trial terms change, so confirm on sonix.ai/pricing before you sign up.

Sonix is an AI transcription platform for uploaded audio and video. Its free trial isn't a time-limited plan with every feature unlocked. It's a small allowance of 30 free minutes of transcription. That's enough to test one short recording, so it pays to know exactly how the minutes are counted before you upload. This guide covers what the trial includes, what it doesn't, where Sonix's own pages disagree, and what you'd pay afterward. For the full plan-by-plan breakdown, see our Sonix review (2026).

How the 30 free minutes work

The Help Center article "How does the free trial work?" lays out the rules:

  • Length of the media, not time spent. Trial minutes count against the total duration of what you upload. Sonix's pricing FAQ says the same about paid hours: "per hour" means the length of the file, prorated to the second.
  • Longer files get cut off. Sonix's example: a 35-minute file uses all 30 trial minutes, and you get only the part of the transcript that fits. To get the rest, you have to buy credits or subscribe.
  • Several short files are fine. You can upload more than one file, as long as their combined length stays within 30 minutes.
  • Standard features are included. Sonix says the trial lets you try standard transcription features such as speaker labels and transcript editing. Its FAQ goes further and says you get "the full Sonix platform including our editor, export options, and collaboration features."
  • Extras use up minutes too. Alignment and burned-in subtitles also count toward your trial minutes.
  • Translation is separate. Transcription and translation each use minutes. If you spend all 30 minutes transcribing, translating that transcript needs paid hours.
  • AI Analysis: three tries. Summaries and similar AI Analysis are available for three attempts during the trial.
  • No AI Workspaces. Sonix says AI Workspaces (asking questions across your recordings) need a paid subscription and aren't available on trial accounts.

To get the most from 30 minutes, Sonix itself suggests trimming your file before uploading so you only transcribe the part you need. If you're judging accuracy, use a clip that's typical of your real recordings: same microphone, background noise and number of speakers. Sonix's FAQ says accuracy "depends on audio quality" and claims 85–99% on clear recordings. Its marketing pages say "99% accuracy." Those are Sonix's claims, not ours. Testing your own audio is what the trial is for.

Do you need a credit card? Sonix's pages disagree

This is the biggest inconsistency we found. On November 25, 2026:

  • The pricing page says "30 min free trial · No credit card required" and "Setting up your account takes just a minute. No credit card required." The FAQ says "no credit card required to start," and the sign-up page (sonix.ai/invite) says "No credit card required • 30 minutes free transcription."
  • The Help Center article "How does the free trial work?" answers "Do I need a credit card to access the free trial?" with "Yes, a credit card is required to access the free 30-minute trial," to prevent misuse. The article "When does my free trial expire?" also says "Sonix requires a credit card to begin the trial."

Both Help Center articles say the card isn't charged unless you buy hours or subscribe, and that you won't be billed automatically when the trial ends or the 30 minutes run out. We can't tell you which version is current, because we didn't sign up. Expect that you might be asked for a card.

Does the trial expire, or just run out?

Sonix's pages disagree here too. The FAQ in "How does the free trial work?" says the trial is 30 minutes "rather than a time-based expiration." But "When does my free trial expire?" says the trial period "is limited and will expire, regardless of usage." It also says Sonix emails a reminder 3 days before the trial ends, or 10 days after sign-up if you haven't uploaded anything. That article doesn't give a fixed length and suggests asking support if you're unsure. In practice, plan to use your minutes soon after signing up.

What happens when the trial ends or your minutes run out

  • New uploads and transcription pause. Sonix says you can still open and review the transcripts you made during the trial. Its Help Center also says your transcripts and uploaded files stay in your account after the trial minutes are used.
  • No automatic charge. Upgrading is optional. If you don't upgrade, Sonix says your access to transcription simply ends.
  • Upgrading during the trial ends it. If you upgrade before the trial is over, Sonix says the trial ends immediately and your paid plan starts. (The article still uses older plan names, "Standard or Premium.")
  • Leftover free minutes are applied first. The pricing FAQ says that if you buy minutes while you still have free ones, the free minutes go to your first upload. Sonix's Help Center example: buy 2 hours on Pay As You Go, upload a 1-hour file, the 30 free minutes cover part of it, and 1.5 hours of credit remain.
  • Stopping emails or deleting the account. Sonix says trial users can delete the account from the account menu to stop emails. Deletion is permanent and only an admin can do it, so download anything you want to keep first.

Other things worth knowing before you start

  • No refunds. Sonix's refund-policy article says all purchases, both transcription credits and subscription fees, are final, including when audio had background noise or overlapping voices. It tells you to use the free minutes to check how Sonix handles your audio before you pay.
  • Cancelling a subscription later. Sonix says the subscription stays active until the end of the billing cycle, then the account returns to trial status. Transcripts stay accessible, but media files are permanently compressed unless you pay for the Storage Plan ($5 per month per 50 GB or part of it).
  • Referral minutes. Sonix's Help Center describes a referral program: when someone signs up with a referral link and subscribes to a paid plan, both people get 100 free minutes. The minutes are added only after the new user pays for Core, Advanced, Pro or Enterprise, not at sign-up. Our Sonix link is a partner link. We haven't confirmed whether it qualifies for those 100 minutes, so don't count on them.
  • Discounts. The pricing FAQ mentions discounted pricing for students, teachers, faculty and non-profits, plus a discounted subscription if you write a 300+ word online article about Sonix.

Other inconsistencies on Sonix's pages

  • Several Help Center articles still use older plan names ("Standard," "Premium") next to the current Pay As You Go / Core / Advanced / Pro names on the pricing page.
  • The FAQ says enterprise plans are for organizations with "100+ hours of content," while the pricing FAQ says to contact the enterprise team if you transcribe "over 1,000 hours per year."
  • The FAQ mentions "Business and Enterprise plans" for advanced sharing permissions, but the pricing page lists no Business plan.

Is the Sonix free trial enough to decide?

It's enough to answer one question: does Sonix's transcript of your audio need more editing than you're willing to do? With no refunds, that's worth answering before you pay. It's not enough for a long interview, a translation test plus a transcription test, or trying AI Workspaces.
