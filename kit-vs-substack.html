Kit vs Substack (2026): fees, free plans and who should use which for a paid newsletter

Research-based comparison. Fees and plan details read from Kit's and Substack's own pricing and help pages on November 16, 2026. Not hands-on tested. Fees change; confirm on kit.com/pricing and Substack's cost page before you decide.

If you want to start a newsletter, and maybe charge for it, Kit and Substack are two of the most common choices. Both let you start for $0. The big difference is how each one makes money from you: Substack takes a share of your paid subscription revenue, while Kit charges a per-sale fee and sells monthly plans for its automation features. This guide puts the fees side by side, works through what you'd keep at a few common prices, and covers what each free option leaves out.

Everything here comes from the two companies' published pages. We haven't run a paid publication on either platform for this article, so it compares what they publish, not how they perform.

The short answer

  • Substack is free to publish on no matter how many subscribers you have. If you turn on paid subscriptions, Substack keeps 10% of each payment, and Stripe charges its own fees on top (2.9% + $0.30 per US card payment plus a 0.7% Billing fee on recurring payments).
  • Kit's Free plan is $0 up to 10,000 subscribers and includes paid newsletters and digital products. Kit says selling in USD costs 3.5% + $0.30 per transaction, with no monthly commerce fee, and calls that the only cost of selling through Kit.
  • On published fees, you keep more of each paid subscription on Kit. Substack's case is that publishing is free at any list size, while Kit's Free plan stops at 10,000 subscribers and automated email sequences need a paid Kit plan (Creator starts at $39 a month at 1,000 subscribers).

Substack math: 10% Substack fee + 2.9% + $0.30 Stripe card fee + 0.7% Stripe Billing fee. Kit math: 3.5% + $0.30. Figures rounded to the cent. Substack's own help page gives the same $8.34 result for a $10 monthly subscription.

At 100 paid subscribers paying $10 a month, that works out to about $834 a month kept on Substack and about $935 on Kit's Free plan, a gap of roughly $101 a month. The gap grows with your revenue, because Substack's main fee is a percentage.

The comparison changes if you need Kit's paid features. If you upgrade to Kit's Creator plan for automated sequences, that's $39 a month (or $390 a year) at 1,000 subscribers, and more at larger list sizes. At small paid revenue, Substack's 10% can cost less than a Kit subscription; as paid revenue grows, the flat Kit plan becomes the cheaper side. Run the numbers at your own price and list size.

What the free options include

Kit Free

Kit's help center lists these on the Free plan: up to 10,000 email subscribers, unlimited forms and landing pages, unlimited broadcasts, tagging and segmentation, digital products and subscriptions, A/B testing on broadcasts, free and Smart Recommendations through Kit's Creator Network, and API access. Support is available on Free too, answered within 24 hours in the order requests arrive.

What needs a paid Kit plan: visual automations and the automation template library, email sequences, rules and RSS campaigns, AI Connectors, paid apps in the Kit App Store, and earning from Paid Recommendations. When you pass 10,000 subscribers, Kit says you need to upgrade to keep sending.

Substack

Substack says publishing is free regardless of subscriber count, and it only earns when you turn on paid subscriptions. For new subscribers, its help center describes welcome emails you can customize for four groups: free, paid, imported and founding subscribers. We didn't find multi-step email sequences or visual automations described in Substack's help pages, so if an automated welcome series or sales funnel matters to you, Kit's paid plans are built for that.

Owning your list

Both platforms let you leave with your subscribers. Substack says you always own your readers' email addresses and can export them as a CSV from the Subscribers page at any time, though it notes that exporting subscribers' full names isn't currently possible. Kit lets you import an existing list onto any plan, including Free, as long as you stay under 10,000 subscribers to remain on Free.

Who should pick which

  • Pick Kit if you plan to charge and want to keep more of each payment, if you also want to sell digital products from the same account, or if you expect to want automated welcome sequences and funnels later. Start on Free and upgrade only when you need automations.
  • Pick Substack if you want to publish for free at any list size without ever paying a monthly bill, you're fine with a percentage of paid revenue going to the platform, and simple welcome emails cover your needs.
  • If you're unsure, work out what you'd pay each platform at your realistic price and number of paying readers a year from now, using the fee table above.

FAQ

Can I run a paid newsletter on Kit's free plan? Yes. Kit's help center says Commerce, including paid newsletters and subscriptions, is available on every plan, including Free, at 3.5% + $0.30 per USD transaction.

Does Substack charge a monthly fee? No. Substack says publishing is free at any size. It keeps 10% of paid subscription payments, and Stripe's processing fees apply.

Is Kit's Pro discount still running? Kit's pricing page currently shows 25% off the first year of annual Pro for lists up to 75,000 subscribers (10% off up to 500,000), and says the offer ends November 30, 2026. Pro renews at the standard annual rate. See our Kit pricing guide for every tier.
