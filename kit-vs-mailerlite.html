Kit vs MailerLite (2026): pricing, free plans and who each one suits

Research-based comparison. Sources checked October 29, 2026. Not hands-on tested.

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) and MailerLite are two of the most common first email platforms for creators and small businesses. They look alike from the outside: both have free plans, both sell digital products and both have visual automation builders. The differences show up in the details. They have very different free-plan limits, different ways of charging as your list grows, and different fees when you sell.

This comparison uses each company's official pricing page and help docs, which are linked throughout. We haven't run campaigns on either platform for this article. We compare what each vendor publishes, and we point out where the published numbers are unclear.

The short answer

  • Choose Kit if you're a creator (newsletter writer, course seller, YouTuber, author) and want a free plan that holds a large list while you grow. You also get creator-focused monetization tools: paid newsletters, tip jars, paid recommendations and a sponsor network.
  • Choose MailerLite if you want a lower starting price on paid plans, a website and landing page builder in the same tool, and pricing that charges for list size and sending volume rather than locking features behind tiers.

Free plans: the biggest difference

The free plans are where the two tools differ most. Kit's pricing page says its free plan is "for creators just starting out" and covers up to 10,000 subscribers. MailerLite's free plan covers 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails, with 2 user seats.

MailerLite's free plan does include more building blocks. Its pricing table lists 1 website plus 1 landing page, 3 active forms, 3 active automations with up to 5 steps each, and one free digital product or booking. MailerLite also says plainly what happens when a free account goes over 250 active subscribers: sending (campaigns and automations) is locked until you upgrade or remove subscribers.

In practice, a creator who expects a list of a few thousand people and doesn't need advanced automation can stay on Kit's free plan much longer. A small business that wants a simple site, a signup form and a welcome email series for a few hundred contacts can do all of that on MailerLite's free plan.

Paid pricing: how each one scales

MailerLite

MailerLite says outright that "pricing is based on usage, not feature access." Its FAQ says every paid plan gets the full feature set, and you pay more for a bigger list or more sending. Comfort starts at $12/month. It includes 50 automations, 10 forms, 10 sites, 5 digital products and 3 seats. Its monthly sending limit is 10× the top of your subscriber tier, so the up-to-1,000 tier gets 10,000 emails a month. Power starts at $25/month and adds unlimited seats, automations, forms, digital products, monthly emails (subject to fair use) and 24/7 live chat support.

A billing detail to know: MailerLite counts subscribers cumulatively within each billing cycle. Any address that was active at any point in the cycle counts toward your limit, even if you delete it later. If you have a paid plan and go over your tier, MailerLite says it automatically moves you up a tier and charges the higher price.

Kit

Kit has two paid tiers, Creator and Pro, and the price for each depends on list size. Kit's pricing page lists what Pro adds over Creator: subscriber engagement scoring ("Subscriber Signals"), engagement analytics, an insights dashboard, deliverability reporting, A/B tests on subject lines and content, a newsletter referral system, Facebook custom audiences, the ability to edit links in sent broadcasts, collaborative editing, unlimited users and 24/7 priority email and chat support. The page also mentions quarterly audits from a "Growth Concierge" team on higher tiers.

At the 1,000-subscriber tier, Kit's entry paid plan was reported at about three times MailerLite's entry price. Where Kit pays off is the creator-monetization features and the large free plan, not raw price per subscriber.

Automation

Both platforms have a visual automation builder with templates. On Kit's pricing page, its visual automation builder, automated sequences, RSS campaigns, an app store with "100+ direct apps," API access and a Kit MCP server for AI assistants appear in its feature list.

MailerLite publishes hard limits per plan: 3 active automations with 5 steps on Free, 50 automations with 100 steps on Comfort, and unlimited automations with 100 steps on Power. Ecommerce triggers, RSS campaigns and auto-resend appear in its comparison table, and multiple triggers per automation are listed as a Power feature.

If you plan to build many separate funnels, MailerLite's 50-automation cap on Comfort is worth noting. Kit doesn't publish an equivalent cap on its pricing page that we could find.

Selling: products, paid newsletters and fees

This is where Kit leans most into creators. Its pricing page lists digital product sales pages and checkout, paid newsletters with gated posts, recurring subscriptions, tip jars, Paid Recommendations (getting paid to recommend other newsletters) and a newsletter sponsorship network. Kit lists a transaction fee of 3.5% + 30¢ on product and subscription sales and says this includes card processing. It lists a 23.5% fee on earnings through Paid Recommendations.

MailerLite also supports selling digital products and bookings, paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions, with Stripe, Shopify and WooCommerce integrations. Its plan limits cap active products and bookings at 1 (Free), 5 (Comfort) or unlimited (Power). We didn't find a per-transaction fee on MailerLite's pricing page, so check its current terms if fees matter for you.

Websites and landing pages

MailerLite includes a website builder with a blog section, landing pages, SEO tools for sites, custom domains and an AI landing-page builder. The number of sites depends on your plan (1 on Free, 10 on Comfort, unlimited on Power). If you don't want a separate website host, this matters.

Kit offers landing pages and forms, and you can remove Kit branding from landing pages, emails and product pages on paid plans. It doesn't present a full multi-page website builder on its pricing page in the way MailerLite does.

Data location and compliance

MailerLite's pricing FAQ says its data is stored in the European Union in an ISO 27001-certified data center, and it describes GDPR controls. It also offers nonprofits a 30% discount on paid plans if they provide proof of status. We didn't find equivalent statements on Kit's pricing page. Check Kit's privacy and security documentation if this matters to you.

Who should pick which

Kit is likely the better fit if you:

  • Run a newsletter or creator business and expect your list to grow past a few hundred people before you earn from it (the free plan goes to 10,000 subscribers).
  • Want paid newsletters, tips, a sponsor network and paid recommendations without adding other tools.
  • Value a built-in newsletter referral system and engagement scoring (Pro).

MailerLite is likely the better fit if you:

  • Want the lowest published starting price on a paid plan ($12/month for Comfort).
  • Want a website, blog and landing pages in the same tool as your email.
  • Prefer to pay for list size and sending volume rather than for feature tiers.
  • Need EU data storage or a nonprofit discount.

How to decide in 30 minutes

  1. Estimate your list size 12 months from now. Enter it into the pricing sliders on both Kit and MailerLite, and note the monthly and annual prices.
  2. List the 2–3 automations you actually need, such as a welcome series, a lead magnet delivery and a product launch. Check them against MailerLite's per-plan automation limits.
  3. If you'll sell products, estimate a month of sales and apply Kit's published 3.5% + 30¢ fee. Compare that with your Stripe fees on MailerLite.
  4. Start on the free plan of whichever one wins, so you don't commit money early.
