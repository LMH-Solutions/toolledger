Kit vs GetResponse (2026): pricing, free plans and who each one suits

Research-based comparison. Prices read from the vendors' live pricing pages on November 6, 2026; plan features and FAQs rechecked November 9, 2026. Not hands-on tested. Prices change; confirm on each vendor's pricing page before you buy.

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) and GetResponse both sell email marketing to creators, but they come at it from different directions. Kit is built around newsletters and creator monetization, with a large free plan. GetResponse is a broader marketing suite: email plus sales funnels, ecommerce automation, webinars, a website builder and a course creator, with a 14-day trial instead of a large free tier.

This article compares what each vendor publishes. We have not run campaigns on either platform for this write-up, and every figure below links back to the official page we checked.

The short answer

  • Choose Kit if you are a newsletter-first creator who wants to grow on a free plan (up to 10,000 subscribers) and later sell paid newsletters, digital products, tips or paid recommendations from the same tool.
  • Choose GetResponse if you are ready to pay and want the lowest published entry price at 1,000 contacts, or you need ecommerce automation and sales funnels (Marketer) or webinars and a course creator bundled with email (Creator).

The trade-off is that Kit's free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers, so a creator who doesn't need paid features may pay nothing on Kit for a long time.

Free plan vs free trial

Kit says its free plan is for building an audience of up to 10,000 subscribers, with unlimited broadcasts, landing pages and forms for one user. Creator and Pro each have a 14-day trial with no credit card required. When a trial ends, you can add a card or stay on the free plan.

GetResponse gives new accounts 14 days of premium features. After that you keep a free account with limits that its FAQ spells out: a GetResponse badge on every message, landing pages capped at 1,000 unique visitors a month, no paid course or newsletter sales, and webinars limited to 10 people. It's a way to evaluate the suite, not a long-term free home for a growing list the way Kit's free plan is.

Automation, ecommerce and funnels

GetResponse Starter includes one custom automation workflow, AI tools limited to three uses, and three users. Marketer adds unlimited workflows, contact tagging and scoring, abandoned-cart recovery, sales funnels, promo codes, revenue reports and web push notifications. If abandoned cart or sales funnels are why you're shopping, Marketer is the plan to look at.

Kit lists a visual automation builder, sequences and integrations across its plans, and Pro adds subscriber engagement scoring, deliverability reporting, an insights dashboard and a newsletter referral system. Kit's pricing page doesn't publish an automation cap that we could find, and it isn't positioned around ecommerce cart recovery the way GetResponse Marketer is.

Selling, courses and webinars

Kit leans into creator income: digital products, paid newsletters, recurring subscriptions, tip jars, Paid Recommendations and a sponsor network. When we checked earlier for our Kit vs MailerLite article, Kit listed a 3.5% + 30¢ transaction fee on product and subscription sales, including card processing.

GetResponse Creator bundles webinars (up to 100 attendees, 3 hours of recording storage), a website builder (up to 5 active sites), a course creator and premium newsletters. Its comparison table lists 0% transaction fees on content monetization, with PayPal and Stripe integrations. If you want live webinars and courses with the same vendor as your email, that's the clearer published match.

Who should pick which

Kit is likely the better fit if you:

  • Want to grow a newsletter to several thousand people before paying anything.
  • Plan to earn from paid newsletters, tips, digital products or recommendations inside your email tool.
  • Want unlimited users and engagement analytics later (Pro), or a free migration when you upgrade.

GetResponse is likely the better fit if you:

  • Are ready to pay and want the lower published monthly price at 1,000 to 10,000 contacts.
  • Sell physical or digital products and need abandoned-cart flows, funnels and promo codes (Marketer).
  • Want webinars, a course creator and a website builder bundled with email (Creator).
  • Qualify for its 50% charity and NGO discount on monthly plans.

How to decide in 30 minutes

  1. Estimate your list size a year from now and enter it on both Kit pricing and GetResponse pricing.
  2. Write down the two or three things you must do: grow free, sell digital products, recover abandoned carts, or run webinars. That usually picks the vendor.
  3. Start free: Kit's free plan or either vendor's 14-day trial, so you aren't paying while you test the workflow.
