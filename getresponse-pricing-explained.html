GetResponse pricing explained (2026): Starter, Marketer, Creator and Enterprise at every list size

Research-based guide. Prices and plan details read from GetResponse's live pricing page (US English, USD) on November 12, 2026 (US Eastern). Not hands-on tested. Prices are net of tax and change; confirm on getresponse.com/pricing before you buy.

GetResponse is an email marketing and automation platform that also bundles landing pages, signup forms and popups, sales funnels, webinars and a course creator. It sells three self-serve plans (Starter, Marketer and Creator) plus a custom-quoted Enterprise plan. Every self-serve plan includes unlimited monthly email sends, and the price is set by how many contacts are on your list. So the honest answer to "how much does GetResponse cost?" depends on your list size, your plan and whether you pay monthly or for 12 months up front.

Everything here comes from GetResponse's own pricing page, plan comparison table and FAQ. We haven't run a paid GetResponse account for this article, so it covers what GetResponse publishes, not how well its emails land or its automations perform.

The 14-day trial and the free account

GetResponse's FAQ says a new Free account gets trial access to premium features for the first 14 days, and the page says no credit card is required. If you don't upgrade, you lose the premium features but can keep using the free version. The FAQ lists these limits on the Free account:

  • A GetResponse badge is added to every message you send.
  • Landing pages are unlimited, but visits are capped at 1,000 unique visitors a month, and A/B testing is paid-only.
  • You can build automation workflows, but the dynamic segment filter needs a paid plan.
  • Webinars are limited to 10 people, including you and presenters.
  • You can build courses for up to 250 students and premium newsletters with free subscriptions, but selling courses or paid newsletter subscriptions needs a paid plan.

The pricing page doesn't state a contact limit for the free account, so check the signup flow if that matters to you.

Starter: list building and newsletters

Starter is $19 a month at 1,000 contacts ($15.58 a month on the 12-month plan). The plan card lists unlimited monthly sends, AI content generators, a welcome email series, 1 custom automation workflow, the landing page builder, signup forms and popups, and a free custom domain on the yearly plan. The comparison table shows 3 users, and the FAQ says AI tools are limited to 3 uses on Starter.

The one-workflow limit is the main thing to weigh. If you only need a welcome series and regular newsletters, Starter covers it. If you want several automated sequences, you'll be looking at Marketer.

Marketer: automation and ecommerce

Marketer is $59 a month at 1,000 contacts ($48.38 on the 12-month plan). On top of Starter it lists unlimited automation workflows, advanced audience segmentation, abandoned cart recovery, sales funnels, promo codes and revenue reports, and unlimited web push notifications. The comparison table shows 5 users. Note the jump: at 1,000 contacts Marketer costs about three times Starter, but at 10,000 the gap narrows to $114 vs $79 a month.

Creator: courses, webinars and paid newsletters

Creator is $69 a month at 1,000 contacts ($56.58 on the 12-month plan). It includes everything in Marketer plus webinars, a website builder, a course creator and premium (paid) newsletter subscriptions. The plan card says the course creator covers up to 500 students at the 1,000-contact tier; the page's price data raises the student allowance with list size (1,000 students at 2.5k contacts, up to 40,000 at 100k). The FAQ says Creator webinars cover up to 100 attendees, and the comparison table lists 0% transaction fees on courses and 5 users.

Enterprise: custom quote

Enterprise has custom pricing; you book a demo to get a quote. GetResponse lists a dedicated sending domain, a dedicated IP address, premium priority support, single sign-on, SMS marketing, mobile push notifications, AI product recommendations and transactional emails on top of the Creator features. It also offers monthly, 12-month and 24-month billing, and a 30-day Pilot Program to test Enterprise tools.

Things to check before you buy

  • List-size brackets: you pay for the bracket your list falls into, so 1,100 contacts costs the same as 2,500. The FAQ says you can upgrade list size, billing period or plan at any time from Billing, and an upgrade starts a new billing cycle.
  • Downgrades: the FAQ says downgrades (to a lower plan, or from annual to monthly) are done only by the support team on request.
  • Payment: payments are automatic, recurring and in advance; the 12-month plan is charged in full up front.
  • Taxes and currency: listed prices are net; GetResponse says you're charged in USD or EUR plus any applicable taxes.
  • Nonprofits: GetResponse advertises a 50% discount on every monthly plan for charities and NGOs.
  • Promotions: none was showing when we checked, but GetResponse's page text references a past Black Friday promotion, so seasonal discounts may appear. Check the live page.

Which GetResponse plan fits

  • Just starting a list, or newsletters plus a welcome series: Starter ($19 a month at 1,000 contacts, $15.58 on the 12-month plan).
  • You sell online and want several automations, abandoned cart emails or funnels: Marketer.
  • You sell courses, run webinars or want paid newsletter subscriptions: Creator.
  • Over 100,000 contacts, or you need a dedicated IP, SSO or SMS: Enterprise.
