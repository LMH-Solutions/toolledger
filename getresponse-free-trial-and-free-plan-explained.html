GetResponse free trial and free plan explained (2026): what you get for 14 days, and what's left after

Research-based guide. Everything here was read from GetResponse's live pricing page, its free trial page and two GetResponse Help Center articles (US English, USD) on November 23, 2026 (US Eastern). Not hands-on tested. Terms change, so confirm on getresponse.com/pricing/trial before you sign up.

GetResponse doesn't sell a "free plan" as a plan card on its pricing page, so a lot of people searching "GetResponse free plan" or "GetResponse free trial" end up confused. In practice they're the same sign-up. You open a Free account. For the first 14 days it has trial access to GetResponse's premium features. After that, unless you upgrade, it drops back to a limited free account that GetResponse's Help Center calls "forever free."

This guide covers what that account includes during the 14 days, what stops working afterwards, and what upgrading costs. It reports what GetResponse publishes, and it points out where GetResponse's own pages disagree with each other.

How the 14-day trial works

GetResponse's free trial page says you can "try premium features free for 14 days" with "no credit card required" and "no commitment." Its pricing FAQ adds that you can upgrade your Free account to a paid plan at any time. A Help Center article says GetResponse has offered this Free account since September 2021.

During those first 14 days the Help Center says you get premium features, which it describes as including "scheduling newsletters, sending autoresponders, publishing automation workflows, using conversion funnels, conducting webinars, creating websites, and more." It's not the same as a paid plan, though. GetResponse lists these limits even during the trial:

  • Contacts: up to 500.
  • Messages: up to 2,500 a month, counted across all message types. A separate help article says this limit applies "to the 14-day free accounts with premium features, as well as the free for life accounts."
  • AI tools: the AI Subject Line Generator and AI Message Generator can be used 3 times, and the website builder's AI generator is limited to 3 uses.
  • A/B tests: not available on Free accounts, for either emails or landing pages.
  • Badge: a GetResponse badge on every message. Removing it requires a paid plan.
  • Landing pages: you can publish as many as you want, but visits are capped at 1,000 unique visitors. Once you hit the cap, the Help Center says your landing pages go offline.
  • Webinars: up to 10 people, including you and any presenters. You can record one webinar of up to 30 minutes and turn that recording into one on-demand webinar.
  • Website builder: you can create as many sites as you want but publish only one at a time.
  • Courses and premium newsletters: the pricing FAQ says you can build courses for up to 250 students and premium newsletters with free subscriptions. Selling courses or paid newsletter subscriptions needs a paid plan.
  • Integrations and API: available only during the first 14 days.
  • Chats: you can put a chat on a GetResponse landing page or link to it from your emails. Embedding chat on your own website needs a paid plan.

Automation workflows, ecommerce campaigns and course and premium-newsletter creation are listed as available during the trial without other limits.

In short, the free plan works as a small newsletter tool for up to 500 contacts. It isn't a free automation platform. If your plan depends on a welcome sequence or other automated emails, those stop on day 15 unless you pay.

Where GetResponse's own pages disagree

We found a few places where GetResponse's pages say different things. We're reporting them as published, not guessing which version is right:

  • "Unlimited sends": the trial page's description mentions "unlimited sends," but the Help Center caps Free accounts at 2,500 messages a month. Unlimited monthly sends is a feature of the paid Starter, Marketer and Creator plans.
  • Landing page visits: the pricing FAQ says Free landing pages are "limited to 1,000 unique visitors per month." The Help Center says the cap is 1,000 unique visits for all landing pages in the account, and that pages go offline once it's reached. It doesn't mention a monthly reset.
  • Landing pages during the trial: one part of the Help Center article says you can "create and publish 1 landing page," and another says you can "create and publish as many landing pages as you want" during the first 14 days. After the trial, both say 1 published page.
  • Support: one help article lists "24/7 live chat in English" during the trial. The pricing FAQ gives English live-chat hours of 7 AM to 11 PM (7 AM to 9 PM on weekends, GMT+1), with a 24/7 chatbot and 24/7 email support. After the trial, the Help Center says chat and the email form are no longer available.

To upgrade, the pricing FAQ says you log in, click Upgrade account in the Profile menu, pick a plan and list size, enter your payment details, and choose monthly billing or the 12-month prepay. Once you upgrade, the Free account ends. Downgrades to a lower paid plan, or from annual to monthly billing, are handled by the support team on request. Going back to the Free account isn't possible.

Other free or discounted options GetResponse lists

  • Enterprise Pilot Program: companies considering Enterprise can book a demo and get 30 days of access to the platform, with dedicated support.
  • Nonprofits: GetResponse advertises 50% off every monthly plan for charities and NGOs.
  • Black Friday / Cyber Monday: when we checked on November 23, 2026, the US pricing page showed regular prices. No Black Friday or Cyber Monday discount was shown, and the page's own data had its seasonal promotion banner switched off. The page's text does include wording for a Black Friday price display, so a sale may appear later. Check the live page before you buy.

How to get the most out of the 14 days

This advice follows from the limits above. It isn't based on our own testing.

  • Test the paid-only parts first. Automations, autoresponders, webinars, the website builder and integrations all stop after day 14. Try those early, while they still work.
  • Keep test contacts to a minimum. Removed and unconfirmed contacts count toward the 500 limit, and GetResponse says the limit can't be reset. Importing a big test list could use up your free allowance.
  • Decide on the plan before you upgrade. You can't go back to Free afterwards, and downgrades go through support.
  • Check that your integration will come back. If you connect a store during the trial, look up GetResponse's restore list. Some integrations, like Amazon, Facebook, Google Analytics, PayPal and Slack, need to be set up again after you upgrade.
