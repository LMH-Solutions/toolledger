4 email marketing platforms for new creators, compared (2026)

Research-based roundup. Sources checked October 29, 2026; Kit slider, GetResponse and MailerLite USD rechecked November 6, 2026. Not hands-on tested. Prices are the vendors' published figures and change often.

If you're starting a newsletter, launching a first digital product or collecting emails for a YouTube channel or blog, your first email platform matters more than it seems. Switching later means exporting lists, rebuilding forms and redoing automations. This roundup compares four platforms that new creators often shortlist: Kit, MailerLite, GetResponse and Brevo.

We didn't run these platforms side by side for this article. Everything below comes from each vendor's pricing page and help docs, which are linked. We focus on the three things that most affect new creators: how far the free plan goes, what the first paid plan costs, and what the pricing is based on (subscribers or email volume).

1. Kit: best for newsletter-first creators

What the vendor publishes: Kit (formerly ConvertKit) says its free plan is "for creators just starting out" and covers up to 10,000 subscribers (kit.com/pricing). There are two paid tiers, Creator and Pro, and you pick a price with a list-size slider. On November 6, 2026 the live slider at 1,000 subscribers showed Creator at $39/month billed monthly or $33/month billed yearly, and Pro at $79/month billed monthly. Pro yearly currently shows a first-year promotional rate of $50/mo ($593 first year) with the standard $66/mo struck through (extra ~25% off first year only; renews at standard—confirm on the page). Kit says yearly billing saves 17%.

Notable features from the pricing page: visual automation builder and templates, automated sequences, RSS-to-email, digital product sales, paid newsletters, recurring subscriptions, tip jars, Paid Recommendations, a newsletter sponsorship network, an app store with "100+ direct apps," an API and an MCP server for AI assistants. Pro adds engagement scoring, deliverability reporting, A/B tests on subject lines and content, a newsletter referral system and unlimited users.

Costs to know about: Kit lists 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction on product and subscription sales (including card processing) and a 23.5% fee on Paid Recommendations earnings.

Good fit if: you're building an audience before you earn from it and want to stay free for as long as possible, or your business is the newsletter itself.

Less ideal if: you need a multi-page website builder, or you're price-sensitive at a few thousand subscribers. Kit's paid tiers start higher than MailerLite's or Brevo's.

2. MailerLite: best value on paid plans

What the vendor publishes: MailerLite's free plan covers 250 subscribers, 2,500 monthly emails and 2 seats. It includes campaigns, 3 automations (5 steps each), 1 website plus 1 landing page, 3 forms and one free digital product or booking (mailerlite.com/pricing). Comfort starts at $12/month (500 subscribers) and $19/month at 1,000; Power starts at $25/month and $39/month at 1,000 (pricing.md, checked November 6, 2026). MailerLite says every paid plan gets the full feature set and you pay more only "for a bigger list or more sending." Yearly billing saves 10%, and there's a 14-day trial of premium features with no card required.

Notable features: drag-and-drop, HTML and simple editors; a website builder with a blog, SEO tools and custom domains; an AI landing-page builder; paid newsletters, recurring subscriptions and bookings; Stripe, Shopify and WooCommerce integrations; an API and MCP server on paid plans. Comfort includes 50 automations and a monthly sending limit of 10× your subscriber tier. Power has unlimited automations and sending, subject to fair use.

Watch out for: subscribers are counted cumulatively within each billing cycle, so deleting contacts mid-cycle doesn't lower that month's count. A free account that goes over 250 active subscribers has sending locked until you upgrade.

Good fit if: you want the lowest published starting price on a full-featured paid plan and want your website in the same tool.

4. Brevo: best when your list is big but you send rarely

What the vendor publishes: Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) prices mainly by email volume, not list size. Its pricing page lists a Free plan, Starter "from 5,000 emails per month," Standard, and Professional "from 150,000 emails per month" with 10 seats included (brevo.com/pricing). The page's structured data lists Starter at $9 and Standard at $18 as starting monthly prices. Brevo's FAQ says the Free plan lets you upload contacts and send "up to 300 emails per day" once your account is approved for sending.

Notable features: email plus transactional messages, SMS (credits sold separately), drag-and-drop editor, AI help with subject lines and copy, MCP access for ChatGPT and Claude, advanced segmentation, A/B testing and send-time optimization on higher tiers. WhatsApp, popups and push notifications come on Professional.

Good fit if: you've collected many contacts (for example from a giveaway or an old blog) but only email them a few times a month. Volume-based pricing can cost much less than per-subscriber pricing in that case.

Less ideal if: you send daily newsletters, since volume pricing grows quickly at high send frequency.

How to choose: a simple decision path

  1. No budget yet, list will grow fast: start with Kit's free plan (up to 10,000 subscribers).
  2. Small budget and you want a website in the same tool: MailerLite Comfort (from $12/mo).
  3. Large list, infrequent sends: Brevo, which is priced by email volume.
  4. You want funnels, landing pages and course tools with one vendor: GetResponse (from $19/mo monthly at 1,000 subscribers).

Work out your own price before you commit

The "starting at" prices above apply only at the smallest tier. Here's a quick way to compare at your real numbers:

  • Write down your expected subscriber count in 12 months and how many emails you'll send each month (subscribers × sends per month).
  • For Kit, MailerLite and GetResponse, enter the subscriber count into each pricing slider.
  • For Brevo, use the monthly email volume instead.
  • Compare annual-billing prices too: Kit lists 17% savings, MailerLite 10%, and GetResponse shows its own yearly figures.

Frequently asked questions

Which has the biggest free plan?

By subscriber count, Kit publishes the largest free plan of these four: up to 10,000 subscribers. Brevo's free plan limits daily sending (300 emails/day) rather than the number of contacts you can store. MailerLite's free plan is 250 subscribers.

Can I switch later?

Yes. All four let you import contacts, and Kit advertises free migration help from an in-house team. Automations and templates usually have to be rebuilt by hand.
