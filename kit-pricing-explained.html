Kit (ConvertKit) pricing explained (2026): Free, Creator and Pro at every list size

Research-based guide. Prices and plan details read from Kit's live pricing page on November 10, 2026. Not hands-on tested. Prices change; confirm on kit.com/pricing before you buy.

Kit, the email platform that used to be called ConvertKit, has three plans: Free, Creator and Pro. The paid plans are priced by how many subscribers you have, so "how much does Kit cost?" really means "how much does it cost at my list size?" This guide lays out every published tier up to 25,000 subscribers, explains how yearly billing and the current Pro promotion work, and shows what you give up by staying on the free plan.

Everything below comes from Kit's own pricing page. We haven't run a paid Kit account for this article, so it covers what Kit publishes, not how the product performs.

The short answer

  • Free costs $0 for up to 10,000 subscribers, with unlimited landing pages, forms and broadcasts, but no automated email sequences or visual automations, and only one user.
  • Creator starts at $39 a month billed monthly, or $390 a year ($33 a month) at 1,000 subscribers. It adds automations, sequences, two users and removes Kit branding.
  • Pro starts at $79 a month billed monthly at 1,000 subscribers. Yearly billing is $790 a year, and Kit is currently showing an extra 25% off the first year of yearly Pro, which brings it to $593 for the first year.

Yearly prices are ten times the monthly price, which is what Kit's "Pay yearly—save 17%" label means: you pay for ten months and get twelve. The first-year Pro figures apply the 25% promotion Kit's page showed for lists up to 75,000 subscribers; for larger lists the page showed 10% off instead. Kit says the discount applies only to the first year of Pro, and the plan renews at the standard rate. Kit publishes tiers well past 25,000 subscribers and offers a custom plan above 500,000; use the slider on its page for those.

What the yearly prices look like per month

Kit displays yearly plans as a monthly equivalent. At 1,000 subscribers that's $33 a month for Creator, $66 a month for standard Pro, and $50 a month for Pro in the promotional first year. At 5,000 it's $75 for Creator and $87 for promotional Pro; at 10,000 it's $116 for Creator and $119 for promotional Pro.

One thing stands out: during the promotion, first-year Pro on yearly billing costs only a little more than Creator on yearly billing at 5,000 and 10,000 subscribers ($1,043 vs $890 at 5,000; $1,418 vs $1,390 at 10,000). If you were already going to pay yearly and want any Pro feature, that gap is small. Just budget for the full Pro price at renewal.

What the free plan includes, and what it leaves out

Kit's free plan is generous on list size. It covers up to 10,000 subscribers and includes unlimited landing pages, opt-in forms and broadcasts, a newsletter feed and website, tagging and segmentation, a custom domain for landing pages, and Kit's free creator recommendation network. You can also sell digital products, paid newsletters, recurring subscriptions and tips on the free plan.

What it doesn't include matters more for most people:

  • No automated email sequences or visual automations. A welcome series that goes out automatically after someone signs up needs Creator or Pro.
  • Kit branding stays on your landing pages, emails and product pages.
  • One user only (Creator allows two; Pro is unlimited).
  • No RSS-to-email campaigns, no direct app integrations from the Kit App Store, no scheduling in each subscriber's time zone, and no email version history.

So the free plan suits someone who sends a newsletter by hand and wants to grow first. Once you want an automatic welcome sequence or a lead-magnet funnel, you're looking at Creator.

Creator vs Pro: what the extra money buys

Creator is the plan most small creators land on. It adds unlimited visual automations and email sequences, RSS campaigns, 100+ direct app integrations, Kit's connector for AI assistants (Kit MCP), Paid Recommendations, polls, removal of Kit branding, SMS marketing, and 24/7 email and chat support.

Pro adds tools for an established list or a team:

  • Unlimited users and collaborative editing.
  • Deliverability reporting, an insights dashboard, engagement analytics and subscriber engagement scoring.
  • A/B testing of up to five subject lines or content (Free and Creator test two subject lines).
  • Editing links in emails you've already sent, and a custom link-tracking domain.
  • Facebook custom audience sync, a newsletter referral system (Kit says it includes a free SparkLoop plan), Subscriber Signals audience research, and a Growth Concierge team with quarterly performance audits.
  • Priority 24/7 email and chat support.

If you're a solo creator who doesn't sell sponsorships or run a team, Creator covers the core email work. Pro earns its price when deliverability reporting, more A/B testing, a referral program or multiple team logins would actually change what you do.

Transaction fees on sales

Kit's pricing table lists a fee of 3.5% + 30¢ on digital products and subscriptions on every plan, Free included. Kit says that figure already includes credit card processing. On a $20 digital product, that's $1.00 per sale ($0.70 + $0.30).

Trials, refunds, upgrades and migration

  • Trial: Creator and Pro each have a 14-day free trial, with no credit card required to start. If you don't add a card when it ends, you can stay on the free plan.
  • Refunds: Kit's FAQ says it refunds monthly or annual plans within the first 30 days after you sign up.
  • Growing past your tier: Kit automatically moves you to the next subscriber tier when you go over your limit, so sending isn't interrupted. Plan your budget around the next tier, not the current one.
  • Shrinking your list: downgrades are manual. Kit asks you to email its help team if you clean your list and want a lower tier.
  • Sending limits: Kit says there are no sending limits on any plan.
  • Migration: Kit offers a free migration service on paid plans, covering subscriber lists and automations.

How to pick a Kit plan in five minutes

  1. Estimate your list size in a year. Kit bills on the tier you're in and upgrades you automatically, so price the tier you'll reach.
  2. Decide whether you need an automatic welcome sequence or funnel. If not, start on Free. If yes, start the 14-day Creator trial.
  3. If you're going to pay anyway, compare yearly Creator with promotional yearly Pro at your tier. While Kit's first-year Pro discount runs, the difference can be small, but the renewal price is the full Pro rate.

On published prices, Kit's paid plans cost more than some competitors at the same list size. GetResponse and MailerLite both list lower entry prices at 1,000 subscribers.
