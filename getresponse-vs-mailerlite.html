GetResponse vs MailerLite (2026): pricing, free plans and who each one suits

Research-based comparison. Sources checked November 6, 2026. Not hands-on tested. Prices change; confirm on each vendor's pricing page before you buy.

GetResponse and MailerLite are two of the most common "all-in-one" email platforms for creators and small businesses. Both sell email, landing pages, automations and digital products. They differ on free access, how plans are priced as your list grows, and how far each tool goes into webinars, courses and websites.

This article compares what each vendor publishes on its pricing pages. We have not run campaigns on either platform for this write-up. Every price and limit below is linked to the official source we checked on November 6, 2026.

The short answer

  • Choose MailerLite if you want a free plan (250 subscribers), a lower published monthly price at the same list size on Comfort vs GetResponse Starter at several tiers, a website builder with a blog in the same tool, and EU data storage. Comfort also keeps paid-plan features available without locking most tools behind a higher tier.
  • Choose GetResponse if you want unlimited sends on every paid plan, sales funnels and ecommerce automation (Marketer), or webinars, a website builder and a course creator in one suite (Creator), and you are fine starting with a free trial rather than a free forever plan.

Free plan vs free trial

MailerLite publishes a free forever plan: 250 active subscribers, 2,500 emails per month, 2 seats, campaigns, 3 automations, 1 website plus 1 landing page, 3 forms and one free digital product or booking. New accounts also get a 14-day trial of premium features with no card required. If a free account goes over 250 active subscribers, MailerLite says sending is locked until you upgrade or remove subscribers.

GetResponse has no free plan card on its pricing page. Its FAQ (rechecked November 12, 2026) says new accounts get 14 days of premium features, after which you keep a limited free account: a GetResponse badge on every message, landing pages capped at 1,000 unique visitors a month, and no selling of courses or paid newsletters. The pricing page doesn't state a contact limit for that free account, so MailerLite's free tier is still the more clearly documented free path.

If you need months of free list-building before you pay, MailerLite (or Kit's larger free plan, covered in our Kit vs MailerLite article) is the clearer published path. If you already know you will pay and want to test funnels, webinars or courses first, GetResponse's trial is the shorter evaluation path.

Automation and ecommerce

GetResponse Starter includes 1 automation workflow, landing pages, forms, limited AI uses (the pricing page lists AI limited to 3 uses on Starter) and 3 users, with unlimited sends. Marketer adds unlimited automation, ecommerce and abandoned-cart tools, sales funnels, web push, advanced segmentation and up to 5 users. That is the plan GetResponse positions for marketers and ecommerce businesses.

MailerLite publishes hard automation caps: 3 active automations (5 steps) on Free, 50 automations (100 steps) on Comfort, unlimited automations (100 steps) on Power. Ecommerce triggers appear across plans in its comparison table; multiple triggers per automation are listed as a Power feature. Comfort's monthly sending limit is 10× the top of your subscriber tier (so the up-to-1,000 tier gets 10,000 emails/month). Power has unlimited emails subject to fair use, plus live chat.

If abandoned cart and sales funnels are the main reason you are shopping, GetResponse Marketer is the plan whose pricing page spells those out most clearly. If you mainly need a welcome series, a few nurture flows and a sending cap you can live with, MailerLite Comfort at the same 1,000-subscriber price as GetResponse Starter may be enough—and Power at $39/mo (1,000) is still below GetResponse Marketer's $59.

Websites, courses and knowledge products

MailerLite includes a website builder with a blog, SEO tools, custom domains (on paid plans) and an AI landing-page builder. Site limits: 1 website + 1 landing page on Free, 10 on Comfort, unlimited on Power. It also sells digital products and bookings (1 / 5 / unlimited by plan), paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions, with Stripe, Shopify and WooCommerce integrations.

GetResponse Creator (from $69/mo at 1,000 contacts) is positioned for creating, marketing and monetizing knowledge products. The pricing page lists webinars, a website builder, a course creator and premium newsletters on top of the Marketer feature set. Starter and Marketer include landing pages; Creator is the tier for the fuller course/webinar stack.

If you want a simple marketing site and blog next to your list, MailerLite documents that path on every paid plan. If you want webinars and a course creator under the same vendor as email, GetResponse Creator is the published match—at a higher monthly price than MailerLite Power at 1,000 subscribers ($69 vs $39).

Who should pick which

MailerLite is likely the better fit if you:

  • Want a free forever plan while the list is under 250 people.
  • Want Comfort's full feature set at $19/mo (1,000) or $49/mo (5,000), without jumping to a $59 Marketer-class plan for basic automation.
  • Need a website and blog in the same tool, EU data storage, or a 30% nonprofit discount.
  • Send a moderate volume that fits Comfort's 10× sending cap, or are willing to pay for Power when you need unlimited sends.

GetResponse is likely the better fit if you:

  • Want unlimited sends on the entry paid plan and do not need a free forever tier.
  • Need ecommerce automation, abandoned cart, sales funnels or web push (Marketer).
  • Want webinars, courses and a website builder in one suite (Creator).
  • Qualify for the charity 50% off monthly plans, or prefer the ~18% yearly discount on GetResponse's published yearly figures.

How to decide in 30 minutes

  1. Estimate your list size in 12 months. Enter it on GetResponse pricing and MailerLite pricing (or use the tables above for 1k / 5k / 10k). Compare monthly and yearly.
  2. List the 2–3 workflows you actually need (welcome series, abandoned cart, product launch). Check them against Starter's single workflow, Marketer's unlimited automation, and MailerLite's 50-automation Comfort cap.
  3. Decide whether you need a free forever plan, unlimited sends on day one, or courses/webinars. That usually eliminates one vendor quickly.
  4. Start on MailerLite Free or a GetResponse trial so you are not paying while you validate the workflow.
